ENG TEACHER — PROJECT VISION AND FUTURE ROADMAP
Evidence-based recovery planning document
Prepared: 17 November 2026 (Asia/Calcutta)

This document extends PROJECT_IDEA.txt. It separates existing code, offline test
evidence, externally dependent validation, and future ideas. Time horizons are
planning scenarios, not promises. The repository audit records final recovered
HEADs and checks; a branch, ZIP, screenshot, or passing fake does not prove a
production feature.

01. WHAT THE PROJECT IS TODAY

Eng Teacher is one monorepo for an AI English-speaking tutor. Its active product
is a Next.js/React web client and a Python LiveKit voice agent. The initial
workflow is a ten-minute Hindi-English mock interview with captions, correction
cards, retries, and a temporary recap. This is a first implementation awaiting
real-provider and physical-device acceptance, not a complete commercial platform.

At discovery, main was 98cfcd7, with earlier organization checkpoints a1457a3 and
38e9d00. It preserved uploaded Cadence and Talking Avatar snapshots under
AnalyseFuture. The remote retry/reference branch at da2796e also contains useful
work. Ten compatible files were consolidated into main at8f1c988, with a
new punctuation/decimal meaning-preservation repair and feature organization.

02. ORIGINAL PURPOSE AND THE OWNER'S EXPANDED IDEA

Help learners who can think in a home language but hesitate in English. A
friendly AI teacher listens patiently, preserves what the learner meant, and
suggests natural English without embarrassment. Eventually an original male or
female animated teacher appears in a video-call-style interface. Clearly label
it as AI, and retain audio-only access. The learner's camera remains optional.

If a learner says "আমি আজ অফিসে যেতে পারব না।", a useful English model is "I
won't be able to go to the office today." Do not invent an illness or change
the negation. The learner can request Bangla help, a repeat, an example, or a
slower explanation before trying again. Bangla examples are product requirements
for review, not claims of validated Bengali speech support today.

03. WHAT HAS BEEN IMPLEMENTED

The active source includes restricted session tokens, pilot-code/origin checks,
microphone publishing, named-agent dispatch, CPU voice-activity detection, OpenAI
speech/text adapters, tutor policy, separate original and suggested sentences,
interruption configuration, session controls, and a fixed ten-minute deadline.
Offline tests exercise browser, schema, tutor, scheduler, and lifecycle behavior.

The last completed main organization checks reported 14 JavaScript, 42 Python,
and six synthetic Chromium tests, plus build, TypeScript, and Ruff. Those numbers
describe that exact earlier run. Recovery changes need fresh evidence. The
recovered retry branch adds meaning-aware retry classification, example/Hindi
translation assistance, question preservation, and recoverable error packets;
these are now recovered on main. Current local validation is14JS/71Python/6
browser checks, build/typecheck/lint/format; provider speech remains unverified.

04. USEFUL WORK AND ASSETS TO RETAIN

Retain the existing voice foundation, tests, lockfiles, provider interfaces,
continuity history, product research, 21-repository inventory, dependency audit,
and preserved snapshots. Cadence contributes structured-exercise design ideas;
Talking Avatar contributes a candidate browser animation SDK. Neither is an
additional running Eng Teacher application merely because its files are present.

Preserve licenses, credits, model/asset notices, and old/new path manifests.
Uploaded branch history and archived material provide recovery evidence. Study
selected components before adoption; do not equate a broad reference application
with permission or need to merge every service and content asset into this app.

05. WHAT IS MISSING OR NOT YET PROVEN

Missing product capabilities include integrated avatar animation, validated
audio-based pronunciation, individual accounts, durable learner-controlled
progress, reviewed lesson catalog, subscriptions, and production operations.
Bengali and other languages lack the required end-to-end acceptance evidence.

Real microphone-to-provider speech, mixed-language quality, natural explanations,
barge-in latency, reconnect, physical Android performance, and billed session
cost remain unverified. Offline passing checks do not close these gaps. Pilot
access code and process-local request limits are not public-service authentication
or distributed quotas. A file audit also cannot prove account security or who
changed a repository.

06. IMMEDIATE IMPROVEMENTS AFTER RECOVERY

Recovery has now preserved the starting versions and consolidated useful retry
changes, with current offline checks recorded in the validation report. Next add
privacy-safe
model-token, provider-failure, and retry totals; avoid transcript or key logging.

This is high-value work of small-to-medium engineering scope, with paid service

07. THE NEXT PRODUCT INCREMENTS

Author one original everyday conversation exercise with a goal, examples,
practice target, ordered turns, help transitions, and teacher-reviewed acceptance.
Use the existing agent and typed events. Then trial one Bengali-English scenario
with fluent reviewers and real audio, including names, numbers, negation, and
mixed sentences. Do not enable an "all languages" switch.

Trial one optional avatar after the speech loop is stable. Select only remote
teacher audio and one playback owner; Talking Avatar plays audio itself, so
leaving LiveKit playback active would duplicate sound. Check interruption,
autoplay, mute, disconnect, and fallback. These are medium-effort increments;
their costs include content review and ordinary phone testing.

08. ADVANCED AI FEATURES WITH PRACTICAL VALUE

Possible later additions are adaptive lesson selection, meaning-aware feedback,
retrieval of learner-approved saved phrases, spaced review, bounded conversational
memory, multilingual explanations, and provider selection using measured quality
and cost. Each feature needs clear inputs, expected behavior, failure fallback,
and evaluation separate from the model's own opinion.

Audio-based pronunciation may provide word/phoneme practice when calibrated
against teachers. Ask for consent and explain uncertainty. An automatic score
must not become a disguised transcript-similarity grade. More autonomy or a larger
model is useful only if learning quality, reliability, or operating cost improves.

09. RESEARCH QUESTIONS TO ANSWER

Which recognition/synthesis combination best handles the target Hindi-English
and Bengali-English tasks? What causes wrong negation or name changes? Does a
visual teacher improve return use or distract from listening? What endpointing
settings accommodate slow speakers without excessive delay? Which pronunciation
errors can reviewers consistently identify across accents?

Compare candidates on the same consented, held-out samples. Separate development
and evaluation speakers. Record failures as well as averages. The initial 30
mixed-language plus 20 already-acceptable utterances per trial is a useful small
gate, not evidence of population-level reliability. Expand samples before making
broader claims, and retain only data whose permissions cover the purpose.

10. WHAT IS FEASIBLE WITH PRESENT TECHNOLOGY

Today a small team can build the bounded voice lesson, original structured
exercises, warm turn-taking, selectable licensed voices, clear correction cards,
experimental multilingual help, simple animated characters, and secure opt-in
practice history. Existing hosted services reduce initial infrastructure work.

Feasible does not mean implemented or free. A web and voice engineer can deliver
small increments; educators and learners must validate whether the teacher is
right and useful. Start narrow enough that failures are understandable. Keep
audio-only and text alternatives so rendering or microphone failures do not
erase every useful route through the product.

11. FEATURES NEEDING MORE RESOURCES

Production accounts, deletion/export, distributed spending limits, monitoring,
backups, and billing require backend/operations expertise and an ongoing hosting
budget. Reliable multilingual content requires paid native-speaker and educator
time. Physical device testing needs actual devices and networks, not viewports.

Pronunciation research needs permitted audio, human labeling, suitable models,
and possibly GPU evaluation. Self-hosted recognition, synthesis, or language
models add deployment, concurrency, maintenance, and license review costs.
Fine-tuning needs a quality dataset and baseline comparisons before GPU spend.
Cost estimates should be measured in service usage and staff effort; no old
illustrative per-lesson price should be presented as a current quotation.

12. EXPERIMENTAL FEATURES

Treat rich Bangla code-switching, meaning-aware correction across dialects,
phoneme scores, personalized model adaptation, automatic lesson-level selection,
and cross-provider recovery as controlled experiments until tested. Some can
work well on selected tasks while failing outside them. Show an experimental
label and a safe escape path rather than quietly treating a model output as truth.

Reproducible experiments need pinned models, test fixtures, a baseline, budget,
human review, stop conditions, and rollback. Review upstream component/model
terms independently. A declared project-level MIT license does not settle every
weight, voice, dataset, content pack, or dependent library obligation.

13. SPECULATIVE DIRECTIONS

Photorealistic interactive video, comprehensive offline multilingual tutoring on
cheap phones, fully automatic educational personalization, and emotion-aware
teaching may become more capable over time. They should remain research options,
not release commitments. Webcam emotion or intent inference is especially
uncertain and may introduce privacy burden without improving learning.

Review such directions annually against real device performance, independent
evaluation, user demand, rights, and total cost. Future model progress may reduce
cost or error; it does not remove ambiguity, consent requirements, or the need
to test with the actual learners this product intends to serve.

14. WHAT IS INFEASIBLE AS AN UNCONDITIONAL PROMISE

Perfect understanding of every language/accent/noisy utterance; knowing true
intent when audio is ambiguous; exact pronunciation grades from text alone;
zero latency and failures; unlimited high-quality inference at zero cost; or
guaranteed fluency, jobs, exam results, and accent changes on a fixed schedule.

Use supported-language boundaries, clarification, audio-grounded assessment,
measured performance, quotas, and specific learning goals instead. AI is not a
real human friend and should not impersonate one. Real-person likeness or voice
cloning without appropriate rights is not an acceptable shortcut. Ownership of
the app also does not imply ownership of every underlying model or service.

15. ONE-YEAR DIRECTION

Aim to prove one reliable adult-learner voice workflow, then a reviewed Bengali
pilot and a small original course. Deliver robust help/retry and recoverable
errors, account controls before durable history, spending safeguards before
public access, and an optional tested lightweight avatar.

Success depends on educator acceptance, real session completion, reproducible
device checks, meaning preservation, and evidence of practice transfer. Earlier
targets such as 27/30 preserved meanings or at most 1/20 false corrections are
starting hypotheses, not achieved results. Refine thresholds with reviewers.
Do not spend the year collecting unrelated reference applications or rebuilding
the transport in a new language.

16. TWO-YEAR DIRECTION

If the core shows learner value and sustainable economics, expand selected
language pairs and levels, learner-controlled spaced review, educator tools,
accessibility, and an operations-supported service. Compare a native mobile
client only when browser constraints or user behavior justify its maintenance.

Benchmark licensed open models against hosted models on equal quality, latency,
and concurrency conditions. Introduce independent hosting or adaptation only
where results justify the staff and compute. Better control of the teaching
policy, content, tests, and provider interfaces is already useful independence;
owning a GPU fleet is not a prerequisite for owning the product.

17. THREE-TO-FIVE-YEAR OPTIONS

With demonstrated demand, consider broader languages, educator partnerships,
calibrated pronunciation tools, richer roleplays, and selected offline practice.
Investigate interactive video only if it provides measured benefit at acceptable
cost. Continue annual checks of models, asset rights, privacy, and accessibility.

The responsible team may grow from owner, application engineer, voice engineer,
designer, and bilingual reviewers to include ML evaluation and operations
specialists. Timelines depend on those resources. Do not promise a future
breakthrough date, an exhaustive global language list, or autonomous teaching
that removes the need for educators and human outcome evaluation.

18. WHAT NOT TO PURSUE NOW

Do not replace the working Next.js/LiveKit/Python foundation just to use one
programming language. Do not wire in whole Cadence services, a second voice
transport, several avatar engines, a new mobile stack, payments, and training
at once. Do not copy competitor branding or curricula. Avoid fabricated
pronunciation/fluency scores, unconstrained subscriptions, hidden recording,
surveillance-based personalization, and unsupported "all languages" marketing.

Do not treat archived material as executable setup instructions. Do not delete
uncertain versions or force-push shared history as a recovery method. Clear
ownership of active code, explicit provenance, and narrow validated improvements
are more valuable than making the repository appear smaller through data loss.

19. THE LONG-TERM PRODUCT ADVANTAGE

The strongest potential advantage is evidence of respectful, meaning-preserving
teaching for specific learners: fewer false corrections, useful home-language
help, preservation of regional English, and next-day use of practiced patterns.
A reviewed curriculum and permissioned evaluation corpus are harder to replace
than a generic avatar or a prompt around a commodity model.

Build trust through visible uncertainty, correct data handling, reproducible
quality tests, stable calling, and reliable cost controls. Durable advantage
comes from accumulated learning research and execution, not proprietary claims
over upstream code or data collected without permission.

20. DEPENDENCIES, EFFORT, COST, VALUE, AND DECISION RULES

Near-term fixes and metrics: small/medium effort; application/voice engineer;
limited service spend; high diagnostic and reliability value. Gate: existing
regressions pass and real failures are observable without exposing learner data.

Original exercise and language pilot: medium effort; educator, native reviewer,
engineer, adult testers; service/review costs; high teaching value. Gate: reviewed
meaning and correction outcomes on held-out tasks, plus usable real calls.

Avatar: medium integration and ongoing device-support effort; browser/audio
engineer and asset review; download/GPU/battery costs. Gate: one sound owner,
fallback, interruption, and mobile acceptance, with an observed learner benefit.

Accounts/retention/operations: medium/high effort; backend, security/operations,
product owner; ongoing hosting/support costs. Gate: authorization, export/delete,
quotas, spending limits, monitoring, and clear provider retention responsibilities.

Pronunciation/self-hosted models: high or uncertain effort; ML evaluator,
educators, permitted data, compute, and operations. Gate: better held-out results
or sustainable total cost than the baseline, with a documented rollback route.

Before each increment, choose a learner problem, a measurable outcome, owner,
budget cap, dependencies, and stop condition. Measure p50/p90 speech latency,
session failures, complete per-lesson cost including retries, and next-day use of
learned patterns. Save evidence and update the roadmap. An AI coding tool can
help implement checks; it cannot substitute for credentials, consent, real
devices, native-speaker judgment, or demonstrated learning.
